An easy first scene

Begin in Northshire

Create a human character to begin near Marshal McBride. Named NPC continuity and shared Northshire guard reports can be configured by your administrator.

Other races work too. Northshire is simply a convenient place to meet nearby NPCs.

  1. Start the realm.

    Use your realm’s documented startup command. Wait for worldserver-daemon) ready... and the character-coordinator startup line. Whether closing a terminal stops the server depends on your launcher.

  2. Open your normal WoW client.

    Open your 3.3.5a client, log in, select Your character-system realm and create your character. Choose Change Realm if the client remembers the previous realm.

  3. Play visibly.

    If your administrator account has GM mode enabled while testing, return with .gm off and .gm visible on before judging ordinary combat, visibility or conversation.

Fresh means fresh. Orion and Anacea are example names throughout this guide. Create them yourself, or substitute your own names. Legacy history imports depend on your administrator’s migration choices.
02

Find your company

A recurring companion

Use one of your own alts

  1. Create a second character on the same account. Enter the world once, then log out.
  2. Log into your main and type .playerbots bot add Anacea.
  3. Use /invite Anacea if she is not already grouped.
  4. If she is far away, try /w Anacea @summon.

Use .playerbots bot remove Anacea to log her out as a bot before playing her yourself.

A same-faction alt is the simplest starting point. Your own alt keeps the gear and progression you give her.

A chance meeting

Invite someone you find

Right-click a friendly roaming bot’s portrait and choose Invite, or type /invite BotName. They may decline if busy, already grouped or outside their invitation rules.

Roaming population, login rules, location and level ranges are Playerbots settings. Ask your administrator what is enabled on your realm.

Roaming bots manage and refresh their own gear. Use an account alt when you want to raise and equip one character deliberately.

Playing together

Use ordinary party play for quests and combat. You can give companions natural-language orders in party chat or a direct whisper. Explicit commands such as /p @follow, /p @stay and /p @attack remain available without a model call.

Orders that affect the game

Address one companion by name, or say “everyone” for the companions you control. Nearby strangers and another player’s alts do not become yours to command. A full raid can take part in dialogue; ordinary NPCs can speak but do not gain Playerbots gameplay controls.

Try sayingWhat to expect
“Everyone stay here.”A standing order that survives ordinary conversation. “Stay for five minutes, then follow me” is a two-step sequence. A newer conflicting order replaces it. “Keep waiting” preserves the existing order and its timer. Returning to you resumes following by default. If you explicitly request holding after arrival, the hold starts once the companion reaches you. Releasing a hold restores movement.
“Anacea, regroup with me.”Follow, approach a nearby identified unit, regroup, retreat, formation and resume use native movement. No general route planning or remote errands.
“Attack the wolf attacking me.”A visible attacker can be identified without selection. You can also name the nearest matching creature, including an attackable critter: “Take down the nearest deer.” This does not guarantee it can be skinned. Ambiguous targets need clarification. Changing selection after the request does not retarget the accepted order. After a recent attack, “and another one” asks for the nearest fresh matching creature; “what about this one” uses your selection at that new message. If nothing identifies the target, the companion asks you. Pointing at a new selection can answer that question.
“Buff me,” “heal me,” “cleanse me.”The bot needs a suitable known spell, mana, range and a valid target. Resurrection still requires normal acceptance. Native AI handles combat rotations and pets.
“Trade me three spring waters.”Exact owned supplies, through the normal trade window and confirmation. “Can I have that jerky?” requests one unit by default; “some water” still asks how much. “Can you trade with me?” prompts a question about the item and amount; answer “Two waters, please.” Short answers refer to the recent offer or question. A pair can mean two of one item or one each of two different items; the companion should ask if that distinction is unclear. An unavailable item should be explained rather than replaced with another supply. The exact offered items and quantities must appear in the trade window before you accept. Cancelling transfers nothing; a new request can retry the trade. Giving or lending coin through dialogue is not supported. Merely mentioning food or an item does not open a trade.
“Repair your gear, spending at most one gold.”Nearby trainers, repairers and vendors use normal prices and prerequisites. State the total spending limit; say “each” only if every recipient gets that allowance. Missing limits prompt a quote.
“Take the tank role.”Changes tactics. It does not rebuild talents, learn spells or unlock a second spec. Spec switches and explicit talent rebuilding need the normal available choices, trainer and money.
“Share that quest with me.”Uses normal quest eligibility and acceptance. Characters can accept an identified available quest, help with current objectives and invite, join or leave a party. They do not grant quest credit, abandon quests or choose rewards for you.

Usually ask for one action, at most two in sequence. The second runs only after the first succeeds and its target and permissions remain valid. Ready gameplay actions are independent of dialogue reading pauses. A request or spoken promise is not proof the action completed.

If a companion knows you are addressing them but needs an item, quantity or clearer target, they ask in character. A compatible short answer within two minutes completes the pending request; changing the request, leaving the audience, logout or changed command authority cancels it. Nothing happens until the missing details pass fresh checks. The companion can also explain a native action failure. System notices remain for unavailable characters, provider/storage problems and expense accounting.

When a character takes the initiative

Personality can influence a helpful spell, a refusal, a gesture, a short approach or an offer of spare supplies. Supply offers transfer nothing: accept the offer in conversation, then confirm the native trade. A duel is a normal challenge that you may accept or decline. These acts have cooldowns; a greeting should not become a stream of buffs or challenges.

Occasional combat reactions can include companions, hostile creatures and bosses. Openings have a low chance and a shared cooldown, with at most two short public turns. Scripted boss speech takes priority. Creatures still use their native combat mechanics, and beasts need not speak like people.

Training, talents and gear

Account alts gain XP and can equip suitable upgrades from their bags. They still need spells and talents. Near the correct trainer, select it and whisper @trainer, then @trainer learn. Inspect @talents, list builds with @talents spec list, or explicitly use @talents autopick.

Trade gear using the normal trade UI. Then use @e [item link] to equip it. The @maintenance and @autogear shortcuts can grant training or gear; keep them for accelerated tests if you want ordinary progression to remain meaningful.

Loot and bags

@inv shows inventory; @stats shows status. @nc -loot removes the noncombat looting strategy and @nc +loot restores it. In this playtest, Free for All disables grouped bots’ ordinary corpse looting; return to Group Loot when they should collect their own drops. A roaming bot may object to loot settings under its normal anti-abuse rules.

A little room to breathe

Ask your party to spread out while following, or to spread out and hold here. Companions choose separate reachable places, skipping blocked cells. A full raid participates. A tight doorway may have insufficient room; characters should explain failures.

In combat, a quick spread gives a short tactical hold (10 seconds by default) while keeping native combat roles. This does not automatically solve a boss mechanic. An explicit timed hold can last up to one hour; a new movement order replaces its timer.

Set up a pull

Get your group together first. Ask everyone to spread out and wait, and your tank to bring a named or selected enemy back. The tank waits for the requested positions, uses native pulling, and returns to its starting position with the enemy before the waiting group resumes combat.

A tank needs a native pull capability and a reachable, living target. If the pull fails or you cancel it, waiting characters keep their hold until you give another order or its timer expires. There is no invented arrival, teleport or automatic dungeon strategy.

Jokes and performances

Try a playful dance challenge. Companions can agree, negotiate or refuse. A native dance persists briefly and stops with movement, combat or a new order. Text emotes remain separate from the dance animation.

You can ask a companion to put its helmet, chestpiece or outfit in its own bags, then restore the exact saved gear. It needs room and cannot change gear during combat or trade. Bags are not removed. This does not give you the items. Temporarily removed gear is protected from automatic selling, cleanup and upgrades.

A normal logout attempts restoration. The exact restoration record is saved together with native inventory, so committed gear removal remains recoverable after a server restart. Ask to restore the original gear after recruiting the companion again. It uses one free native equipment-set slot and preserves existing sets; if all ten are occupied, removal fails before changing gear. Missing original items cannot be replaced with identical-looking substitutes.

Find help in General

Ask for help in General. A nearby, available companion may volunteer and send a normal party invitation. You choose whether to accept. Once grouped, ask for native quest assistance; it does not grant objectives or rewards.

Channel conversation does not grant authority over distant characters. Volunteering remains optional and does not teleport someone to you, remove them from another player's group, or reveal your private quest log.

What a conversation feels like

A character can speak, perform an emote, then speak again. Longer segments leave more reading time before the next segment or speaker: about 150 words per minute plus a two-second pause (10 words: 6 seconds; 20 words: 10 seconds; 30 words: 14 seconds). Longer lines retain their full reading pause. Replies can take longer to begin. A new message, the start of combat or a changing audience can interrupt the remaining social reply. You can still speak during combat; those replies are brief.

The system chooses who replies next; not everyone has to speak. An exchange allows up to four generated turns. One character finishes their speech/emote sequence, then leaves room for someone else; the same speaker cannot trigger another consecutive reply. Characters can also start occasional public conversations while a human is present, generally after 20–40 minutes of eligible quiet activity. They do not run private bot-to-bot whisper conversations.

Yes-and, with a spine. Characters should accept reasonable scene-building while correcting established contradictions. “Your brother sent me” can produce “I have no brother—but tell me who you met.” A spoken claim is not automatically a true memory.

There is no special OOC editing phrase. “OOC:” in a normal message is still dialogue. Use the explicit .chars controls when you mean to change a record. Generated emotes describe roleplay; they do not grant items, move characters, kill enemies or alter quests.

Native emote scripts take priority. A guard may wave back through its game script without starting a generated conversation. Use ordinary /say to continue, or an unscripted gesture such as /nod.

Companions & named NPCs

A person, not a blank prompt

Character creation does not generate a biography or invent memories. A meaningful first interaction can create a companion biography using native facts. NPCs use prepared identity and compact area/quest records when configured; missing personal history stays unknown. Known facts and your explicit corrections take priority. Identity persists across logout, recruitment and server restart.

Recent conversation, lasting notes and relevant relationships inform future replies. A character may remember something without having said a word at the time.

Generic guards

A watch with a reputation

Configured watches and native player guilds share attributed reports within their memberships. A second guard can know what the local watch heard without claiming to have witnessed it personally.

Generic NPCs have immediate context; they do not each accumulate a named character’s lifetime biography. Watch reports do not automatically change reputation, faction standing or combat hostility.

Make a surgical correction

  1. Select the character.

    .chars info shows their identity and character version. .chars notes shows recent notes with IDs and versions.

  2. Add an authoritative fact—or edit one note.

    Use .chars fact VERSION Anacea has no siblings. for an owner fact. To correct an existing note use .chars edit ID:VERSION Corrected text here. Substitute the displayed numbers.

  3. Check the result.

    Expect a short confirmation. .chars notes lets you inspect the change. If a version is stale, read the current record and retry with that version. Old pending replies are cancelled.

.chars resolve ID:VERSION marks an obligation or report resolved; it is not a delete command. For a bad source event, an administrator can inspect .chars history and .chars episode ID:VERSION, then use .chars revise or .chars exclude. The original version remains stored.

Owners can change their companions’ records. Your local admin account can also inspect NPCs and watches. A fact override may live in the notes alongside an older biography; it takes priority in future context. Administrative inspection and edits do not spend model credit.

06

Know the edges of this world

Ready to use

Characters within the game

  • WotLK 3.3.5a client; level cap and XP rates follow realm configuration.
  • Account alts and roaming bots when enabled by Playerbots.
  • Ordinary quests, combat, trainers, vendors and party mechanics.
  • Character conversations, persistent identity and targeted edits.
  • Optional canonical named NPCs and separate local guard watches, configured through NpcIdentities.
Current limits

What to keep in mind

  • Most NPCs have no researched personal backstory yet; the bulk lore catalogue is future work.
  • Ordinary NPCs talk locally. They do not join your party, whispers or General.
  • Native guild dialogue respects membership and chat rights. Officer and battleground dialogue are not supported.
  • Dialogue actions are limited to supported native abilities. No automatic selling, arbitrary NPC control, free spells, invented items or quest completion.
  • Existing accounts, inventory and history follow the administrator’s migration choices; this module does not create a fresh realm.
  • Models can still misunderstand or repeat themselves. Corrections and the playtest are there to catch that.
Optional realm population

Fresh and familiar faces

The population manager introduces adventurers gradually. It favours occupied regions and nearby levels while retaining veterans elsewhere. Starting an alt creates demand for beginners; it does not lower the levels of people you already know.

Repeated encounters, shared adventures, trades, conversations and your Friends list strengthen a character’s chance of appearing again. Familiarity belongs to your individual character. Your alt does not inherit another character’s relationships.

People you have encountered retain their identity. Established adventurers keep their actual locations; a reunion is not a teleport. Available regional seats mix familiar and unfamiliar people, subject to who can actually be there.

Progression and rest

The world can wait for you

Fewer bots stay online when humans leave. Autonomous activity uses a limited allowance earned during human play, and restarting the server does not refill it. Bots farther from human level ranges get fewer autonomous opportunities.

A familiar companion behind your level can earn up to 50% extra XP while adventuring nearby in your party. It still earns XP from actual gameplay; there is no automatic level match. Parties, combat, trades and unfinished interactions take priority over population rotation.

Administrators can inspect .playerbots population. Online simulation and the reserve roster have separate limits; established dormant characters can remain stored beyond the reserve target. Population scheduling itself makes no AI requests. Dialogue still follows the configured model budget.

Your administrator supplies the current realm progression to the characters. A low-level adventurer should not invent victories over late-game bosses. Level changes are not world-phase changes, and a generated biography is not a quest-completion record.

Client addons, including installed quest voice packs, belong to the client and remain available. Their voices are separate from the new text-based character dialogue.

08

When something feels wrong

I cannot connect, or I see the old realm.

Start the server using your realm’s documented startup command and wait for readiness. Choose Change Realm in the client and select Your character-system realm. The client should keep its local 127.0.0.1 realmlist.

A bot follows me but will not talk.

Whisper a complete sentence without @. Check that it is an online bot, not an ordinary NPC; whisper only works with bots. Give the request time to finish. Use .chars usage to inspect the allowance. A failed provider request can produce silence while preserving what was witnessed.

I cannot find roaming bots.

Arrivals are gradual. Stay in a region for a little while and play with .gm off. The population is spread across the world; not every adventurer is at your spawn. Administrators can check .playerbots population for online counts and whether persistence is healthy. Your own account alt remains a reliable companion for an immediate test.

A bot knows something it should not.

Stop and record the exact channel, speaker, location and approximate time. Select the character and inspect .chars history, then .chars notes. Avoid asking a leading question that reveals the supposed secret. A shared guard report should be attributed to the watch, not remembered as a personal experience.

My correction was refused—or the old description still appears.

Recheck the current version with .chars info or .chars notes. Use the character version for .chars fact, and the note’s ID:VERSION for .chars edit. Fact overrides are separate authoritative notes; the original generated biography can still appear in .chars info. Inspect the note and test a fresh conversation.

There is no spontaneous chatter.

It is deliberately occasional: the configured quiet interval is 20–40 minutes, and someone eligible must be there. Not every quiet pause produces speech. New public conversations require a human audience. There is no bot-to-bot whisper simulation.

A merchant or quest giver will not perform what they said.

Use the ordinary NPC interaction window for quests, buying, selling and training. A generated line such as “I’ll fetch supplies” is dialogue, not a scripted inventory action. Report misleading claims so the prompts can be improved.

What do the automated checks cover?

Native checks exercise dialogue delivery, emotes, interruptions, memory boundaries, commands and gameplay outcomes. Model quality still benefits from your own playtest; a successful build alone is insufficient evidence.

I want to submit a useful bug report.

Include your character and companions, .gps output, channel, exact lines, expected result and what happened instead. A screenshot helps for misplaced emotes or UI. Mention whether it followed combat, movement, a correction or relog. Do not include API keys or database credentials.

Server controls

Use your realm administrator’s documented startup and shutdown commands. Keep live account details, API keys and database credentials outside this guide and source control.
